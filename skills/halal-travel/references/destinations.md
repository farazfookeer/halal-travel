# Choosing a destination

This file holds stable, slow-changing traits. Anything current (rankings, visa rules, safety advice, prices, openings) must be looked up during the session.

## Live checks to run every time

1. **Muslim-friendliness ranking**: fetch the current Global Muslim Travel Index (GMTI) table from CrescentRating (crescentrating.com, rankings page) and note the destination's position. Search "Global Muslim Travel Index [year]" if the page has moved.
2. **Government travel advice** for the traveller's nationality (UK: gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice; US: travel.state.gov). Note any warnings for the region.
3. **Entry rules**: visa, passport validity, any rules affecting the party (for example, travel with minors, unmarried couples' hotel rules in some countries, rules on women travelling).
4. **Weather for the travel month**, including heat during Ramadan if relevant.
5. **Public holidays**, including Islamic holidays from the date check.

## Destination types

### Muslim-majority, halal by default
Halal food is the norm, mosques everywhere, azan audible, Ramadan and Eid lived publicly. Watch for international hotel chains and tourist zones that still serve alcohol and pork.

- **Turkey**: wide range of dedicated halal and "conservative" resorts on the Aegean and Mediterranean coasts with women-only pools, beaches and spas; Istanbul for heritage. Hotel alcohol policy varies a lot; check each one.
- **Malaysia**: strong halal certification (JAKIM), easy for families, prayer rooms in malls and attractions. Langkawi and Penang for beaches.
- **Indonesia**: Muslim-majority, halal by default outside Bali (Bali is Hindu-majority; halal needs checking there). Lombok is often marketed as a halal-friendly alternative.
- **UAE (Dubai, Abu Dhabi)**: halal food standard, mosques and prayer rooms everywhere, but alcohol is widely served in hotels. Dry hotels exist; check.
- **Oman, Qatar, Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait**: quieter, family-oriented; heat in summer.
- **Saudi Arabia**: Umrah plus tourism (AlUla, Red Sea). Check current tourist and Umrah visa rules and Hajj season restrictions.
- **Morocco, Egypt, Tunisia**: heritage, beaches, desert; halal by default in local restaurants; resorts vary on alcohol.
- **Maldives**: local inhabited islands are alcohol-free by law and have mosques; resort islands usually serve alcohol and have mixed beaches. Private pool villas help on modesty.
- **Central Asia and Caucasus (Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan)**: Silk Road heritage; halal widely available but alcohol is socially common.
- **Balkans (Bosnia, Albania, Kosovo)**: Muslim heritage, good value; alcohol is common in restaurants.
- **Brunei**: alcohol not sold; very conservative and quiet.

### Muslim-minority with good halal infrastructure
Halal food available but needs mapping; mosques exist but need locating; Ramadan is not public life.

- **Singapore**: MUIS-certified food widely available; easy.
- **United Kingdom**: large Muslim communities; halal food abundant in cities; many mosques.
- **Japan, South Korea, Taiwan**: halal certification growing; prayer rooms at major stations, airports and some attractions. Map halal food carefully; dashi, mirin and sake are common in Japanese cooking.
- **Thailand**: southern provinces and parts of Bangkok have halal food; Thai Halal certification exists.
- **Spain (Andalusia)**: Islamic heritage (Granada, Córdoba, Seville); halal food in cities, limited elsewhere.
- **Islamic heritage in Europe** (Sicily, Andalusia, Sarajevo): history-led trips with halal food concentrated in specific districts.
- **South Africa (Cape Town)**: strong Cape Malay Muslim community; halal food widely certified.

### Dedicated halal resorts
Mostly in Turkey, with others in Malaysia, Indonesia, the Gulf, North Africa and parts of Europe. These offer the full package: alcohol-free, halal kitchens, women-only facilities, prayer rooms, Ramadan programmes. Use these when the traveller has Strict on most criteria. HalalBooking is the main specialist platform; see `booking.md`.

## Matching destinations to the brief

When the traveller has not chosen a destination:
1. Filter by season and travel time from their origin.
2. Filter by the Strict criteria: if Privacy and Modesty is Strict, prefer dedicated halal resorts or private villas.
3. Rank by Halal Fit (expected), budget fit, and the GMTI position.
4. Offer 3 options with a one-line reason each, plus one "wildcard" if it fits their interests.
