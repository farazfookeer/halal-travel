# Halal criteria and the Halal Fit Score

Use this to judge every property, restaurant, activity and flight. The traveller sets the strictness level for each criterion during intake; never assume the strictest or the most relaxed reading on their behalf.

## Strictness levels

Each criterion has three levels. Record the traveller's choice in the trip brief.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strict | Must be met. A property that fails is excluded, not just scored down. |
| Preferred | Strongly wanted. Failing it costs points and is flagged. |
| Relaxed | Nice to have. Mentioned if present, ignored if absent. |

## 1. Food and alcohol

**Strict**
- Property is fully alcohol-free (no bar, no minibar alcohol, no alcohol served anywhere on site).
- All on-site food is halal certified by a recognised body, or the property is in a Muslim-majority area where meat is halal by default and the property confirms it.
- At least 3 halal dining options within 15 minutes of the base.

**Preferred**
- Alcohol may be served, but not in the main restaurant, pool area or family zones; minibar can be emptied on request.
- Halal meat served at the property (certified or confirmed in writing).
- Halal options reachable easily every day.

**Relaxed**
- Halal or seafood/vegetarian options available nearby; traveller self-manages.

**Evidence to look for**
- Certification body named (e.g. JAKIM, MUIS, HMC, HFA, local Diyanet-linked bodies, ESMA in UAE). A named body beats a self-applied "halal" label.
- HalalBooking or similar platform badges such as "no alcohol" or "halal food", which are platform claims; treat as claimed, not verified.
- Recent guest reviews mentioning halal food or alcohol policy (search reviews for "halal", "alcohol", "bar").
- Muslim-majority location context: in Turkey, Malaysia, Morocco, Jordan and the Gulf, meat is usually halal, but international hotel chains may still serve pork or alcohol. Say so.

**Food notes to pass on**
- Seafood and vegetarian dishes may still be cooked in wine or with non-halal stock; flag cuisines where this is common (French, Italian, Japanese mirin and sake, Chinese cooking wine).
- Gelatine in desserts and sweets; E-numbers such as E120 (carmine) and E441 (gelatine).
- Airline meals: request the Muslim meal (IATA code MOML) at least 24 to 48 hours before departure.

## 2. Prayer logistics

**Strict**
- Mosque with Jummah within a reasonable trip (20 minutes) on every Friday of the stay.
- Prayer room or qibla marker in the property, prayer mats available.
- Itinerary built around all five prayers.

**Preferred**
- Mosque reachable for Jummah; qibla direction known for the room.
- Activities avoid clashing with Jummah.

**Relaxed**
- Prayer times and qibla provided; traveller manages.

**Evidence**
- Property amenity lists ("prayer room", "qibla direction", "prayer mats on request").
- Mosque listings near the base (maps search "mosque" or "masjid" near the address, then check opening and Jummah times on the mosque's own page or social media).

## 3. Privacy and modesty

**Strict**
- Women-only pool, spa and beach areas with no line of sight from mixed areas, or a private pool villa that is not overlooked.
- Female staff in women-only areas.
- Family-only or family-oriented property (no adults-only party scene).

**Preferred**
- Separate swimming times for women, or a private or screened pool.
- Burkini and modest swimwear permitted in all pools (some European resorts ban them; check).
- Quiet, family-oriented atmosphere; no nightclub on site.

**Relaxed**
- Modest swimwear allowed; traveller decides on mixed facilities.

**Evidence**
- Property photos of the women-only areas (screening, walls, roofed sections).
- Pool rules or FAQ pages on swimwear.
- Reviews from Muslim families (search "burkini", "ladies pool", "private pool overlooked").
- Villa listings: check whether the pool is overlooked by neighbouring villas or hotel blocks.

## 4. Ramadan and Eid

See `ramadan-eid.md`. Score whether the property provides suhoor (pre-dawn meal), iftar, Taraweeh arrangements, and Eid prayer access when the trip overlaps.

## 5. Ethics and wider values (optional)

Only score if the traveller asks: ownership and labour practices, riba-free payment options, avoiding destinations or businesses they have chosen to boycott. Ask; do not assume.

## The Halal Fit Score

Score each criterion 0 to 3, weight by the traveller's level, and show the working.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | Fully meets the Strict standard, with verified evidence |
| 2 | Meets Preferred, or meets Strict on claimed evidence only |
| 1 | Partly meets; workaround needed |
| 0 | Fails, or no information |

Weights: Strict = 3, Preferred = 2, Relaxed = 1, Not wanted = 0.

`Halal Fit % = sum(score x weight) / sum(3 x weight) x 100`

Any Strict criterion scoring 0 or 1 means **excluded**, whatever the total.

### Evidence labels

Every halal claim in a shortlist or itinerary carries one of these labels:

- **Verified**: named certification body, the property's own written policy, or written confirmation from the property.
- **Claimed**: platform badge, marketing copy, or a single review.
- **Unknown**: nothing found. Suggest the question to ask the property (see `booking.md` for the email template).

Never upgrade a Claimed item to Verified without a source. Never state that a place "is halal" with no evidence.
